# Working story direction

Current implementation scope ends at the bandit camp. Later developments below
are planning context, not permission to build them or reveal them early.

## Opening through the camp

- The ordinary player lives on Inkwell Road and travels primarily alone.
- Wake → warning bell → masked figure fleeing → help Luma → fight violet
  Blotlings → reach the travellers → Serif refers the player to Bellum.
- Luma reunites with Mira. Inkwell remains a living, recurring home hub, not
  a mission corridor. Some residents discuss the crisis; others have ordinary concerns.
- Neither Serif nor Bellum knows what Blotlings really are at this stage.
- Bellum sends the player to investigate Wordwood. Preserve the word puzzles,
  optional field notes, and seed → sprout → bloom gate sequence.
- Beyond the gate is a second dungeon, the Sunken Repository. Drainage, a guarded
  key and a vocabulary seal lead to the Wordwood Tablet. Pickup uses an overhead
  pose and an original item sting. The Tablet demonstrates violet ink interfering
  with an original inscription without erasing it, not the Blotlings' origin.
- After returning, a missing page from Bellum's research leads back to Inkwell
  Road. Bandits have the page. The player ultimately befriends them and recovers
  it, rather than slaughtering them. The bronze road guard can participate.
- The camp can remain as a permanent point of interest. The theft, evidence,
  encounter, and friendship sequence still need design and implementation.

## Bellum's voice

Bronze-ink village scholar, knowledgeable but not omniscient. Nerdy through
specific curiosity: color distinctions, ordered sequences, observations versus
theories, meticulous labels, and excited self-corrections. He sometimes gets
carried away, notices, and returns to the person in front of him. Warmth comes
before research when someone is in danger. Avoid a joke in every line or making
him incompetent. His name is Bellum, not the legacy Vellum.

## Later planning context — do not reveal early

The recovered page and accumulated evidence eventually allow Bellum to discover
that Blotlings are corrupted Inklings and develop counter-ink. That breakthrough
comes after the camp, not during the opening or Wordwood. Future rescues can
return people to homes in deliberately designed new areas; existing Inkwell
residents are not retroactively missing.

Possible later destinations include Lexoria (King Quill, Queen Sonnet, unnamed
Grand Prince) and an eerie Grand Library midpoint dungeon with an evil Librarian.
Bellum's Archive eventually grows into a laboratory.

The working antagonist concept is a father creating outbreaks to temporarily
stabilize his transforming daughter. His treatment works temporarily, not as a
cure. The eventual goal is stopping the harm while saving her; Bellum develops
a true cure. Exact history, mechanisms, and connective events remain open.

## This pass

Implemented Bellum's early voice and the Wordwood expedition: rainy woodland,
three optional ruin interiors, five Repository rooms, saved Tablet acquisition,
and Bellum's interference research. The seed/sprout/bloom gate remains independent
of Tablet collection. Old completed saves do not automatically receive the Tablet.
No bandit camp, purification system, or later-region content implemented.
